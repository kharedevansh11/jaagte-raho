## Part 2: Customer-discovery call guide

**Goal of every call:** learn how they handle tired drivers at night today, and what it has cost them. Do not pitch. Do not describe the product until the last two minutes, and only if they ask.

**Rules for yourself**
- Ask about the past ("last time...", "last month..."), never "would you...?". Hypothetical answers are polite guesses.
- Talk 20%, listen 80%. Silence is fine; let them fill it.
- If they ask "kya bech rahe ho?", answer honestly: "Abhi kuch nahi bech raha. Main samajhna chahta hoon problem hai ya nahi."
- Write down exact phrases they use. Those are your future marketing words.
- Keep it to 10–12 minutes unless they want to keep talking.
- If the person who picks up isn't the owner/manager, ask: "Night ke drivers ka kaam kaun dekhta hai? Unse 10 minute baat ho sakti hai?" and ask for a good time to call back.

### 1. Opener (about 15 seconds)

> "Namaste, main Devansh bol raha hoon. Main ek chhota founder hoon, aur night-shift drivers ki safety par research kar raha hoon. Main kuch bech nahi raha. Bas 10 minute aapka experience samajhna hai: raat ki trips mein drivers ko jaagte rehne mein kya dikkat aati hai. Abhi baat karna theek rahega, ya baad mein call karoon?"

If they say "busy": "Koi baat nahi. Kal kaunsa time theek rahega, din mein ya shaam ko?"

### 2. Warm-up: context (1–2 minutes)

1. "Aapke paas kitni gaadiyan hain, aur unmein se roughly kitni raat mein chalti hain?"
2. "Raat ki trips kaisi hoti hain: airport, staff drop, ya long-haul? Ek typical raat kaisi dikhti hai?"

### 3. Core questions (past behaviour, Mom Test style)

3. **Current process:** "Abhi raat mein aap kaise ensure karte ho ki driver jaag raha hai? Pichhli raat specifically kya hua tha?"
4. **Last incident:** "Aakhri baar kab hua ki koi driver raat mein so gaya, ya neend ki wajah se accident ya near-miss hua? Kya hua tha, step by step?"
5. **Cost of that incident:** "Us incident mein kitna nuksaan hua: gaadi ki repair, insurance claim, client ka penalty, driver ka ilaaj, gaadi kitne din off-road rahi?"
6. **Who handles it:** "Raat mein drivers ko kaun dekhta hai: aap khud, supervisor, ya control room? Woh raat mein kya kya karta hai?"
7. **Supervisor calls:** "Kya koi drivers ko raat mein phone karta hai check karne ke liye? Kitni baar? Pichhle hafte kitne calls hue? Kaun karta hai, aur usko kitna dete ho?"
8. **What they've tried:** "Aapne pehle kya kya try kiya hai: GPS alerts, dashcam, DMS (driver monitoring) device, chai-break rules, shift limits? Kya chala, kya band kar diya, aur kyun?"
9. **Money already spent:** "Driver safety ke liye pichhle saal kisi cheez par paisa kharch kiya? Kitna, kis par, aur decision kisne liya?"
10. **Drivers' side:** "Drivers khud kya bolte hain jab unse raat mein call karte ho? Unko pasand aata hai ya irritate hote hain? Kaunsi bhasha mein baat karte hain?"
11. **Client pressure** (staff-transport operators): "Aapke corporate clients night-shift safety ke liye kya maangte hain: audits, SOS, escort? Kabhi kisi client ne penalty lagayi ya contract chala gaya?"
12. **Calls while driving:** "Aapke drivers chalti gaadi mein phone uthate hain? Aap khud unhe raat mein call karte ho to woh kaise baat karte hain: Bluetooth, speaker, ya gaadi rok ke? Kabhi laga ki call ki wajah se dhyan bhatka?" *(This tests the distraction objection. Listen for whether they already call drivers while driving.)*
13. **Last question:** "Is topic par aur kya hai jo mujhe poochhna chahiye tha, par maine nahi poochha?"

Pick 8–10 of these, but always ask 12. Skip 11 for cab/truck owners without corporate clients.

### 4. Signals: real pain or polite interest?

**Real pain (strong signals)**
- They tell a specific recent story with a date, a driver, and a rupee figure, without being pushed.
- They already pay for a workaround: a night supervisor, a control-room person making calls, dashcams or DMS devices, extra drivers to cut shift length.
- The owner or manager personally loses sleep or gets 2 a.m. calls over this.
- A client, insurer, or the police has penalised or questioned them over a night incident.
- They ask you "kab tak ready hoga?" or "price kya hoga?" before you mention a product.
- They offer an intro: "Mere ek dost ka bhi fleet hai, unse baat karo."
- They agree to a concrete next step: a date, a named driver list, a WhatsApp group.

**Polite interest (weak signals)**
- "Haan, achha idea hai", "Banao, phir dekhenge."
- Only general statements ("safety important hai") with no specific incident.
- "Hamare drivers experienced hain, unko neend nahi aati."
- They have never spent a rupee or an hour on this problem.
- Compliments without time, money, or an introduction attached.
- "Bhej do details WhatsApp pe" with no follow-up commitment.

After each call, note one line: **pain level (high / medium / none), what they pay today, who decides, next step.**

### 5. Soft close: free pilot

Only after you've listened. Keep it low-pressure:

> "Bahut shukriya, kaafi kuch seekhne ko mila. Main ek simple cheez bana raha hoon: raat mein har 20–30 minute driver ko unki bhasha mein ek chhota phone call jaata hai, aur agar woh bahut neend mein lage ya do call miss kare, toh aapko ya manager ko turant call aata hai. Kya aap 2–3 drivers ke saath ek free pilot try karna chahenge, ek-do hafte ke liye? Koi charge nahi, koi commitment nahi."

- If **yes**: "Badhiya. Kaunse 2–3 drivers? Unki shift timing kya hai? Kis din se shuru karein?" Lock in a date before hanging up.
- If **maybe**: "Kya cheez aapko rok rahi hai?" Listen; don't argue.
- If **no**: "Koi baat nahi. Kya aap kisi aur fleet owner ko jaante ho jinko yeh problem hoti hai?"

Always end with: "Kya main 2–3 hafte baad dobara call kar sakta hoon update dene ke liye?"
